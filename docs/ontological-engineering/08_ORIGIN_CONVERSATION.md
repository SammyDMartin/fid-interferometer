# Origin Conversation: March 29, 2026

## Context
This document records the conversation in which ontological engineering was identified, named, and systematised as a practice. The conversation took place between Sammy Martin and Claude on the evening of March 29, 2026.

## Key Developments in This Conversation

### 1. The Omni-Experiment
Sammy presented his "craziest idea" — build a naked singularity and throw the Earth into it as a hedged bet across every metaphysical worldview simultaneously. The argument: the cumulative probability across physics (information conservation), simulation theory, anthropic reasoning, moral realism, unprobability, and theism that "something ontologically interesting happens" may exceed the probability of "nothing happens," especially since "nothing happens" requires unitarity violation (the outcome physics considers least likely).

### 2. The Unitarity Wedge
The sharper version: there's a genuine gap in physics at naked singularities. Information goes in, unitarity says it can't vanish, there's no horizon for holographic encoding, and physics doesn't tell you where it goes. This gap is the wedge. Every non-default worldview has an answer for what fills it. The thought experiment forces you to put real numbers on how much credence you assign to "nothing interesting happens" versus the alternatives.

### 3. Naming the Practice
Sammy observed that his various proposals (Eucharist hack, improbability drive, unprobability, simulation stress-test, SCP-3812 analysis, Omni-Experiment) share a specific intellectual move: treating framework axioms as engineering specifications and finding exploits. The practice was named "ontological engineering" or "metaphysical engineering."

### 4. The Extended TRL Scale
By analogy with NASA's Technology Readiness Levels and Drexler's exploratory engineering, Sammy proposed negative TRL levels for ideas that sit below the floor of established science:
- TRL 0: Theoretically derived but unobserved
- TRL -1: Extrapolation from contested but coherent frameworks
- TRL -2: Extrapolation from frameworks whose coherence is uncertain
- TRL -3: Mythic intuition / fairy-tale level (category errors that might not be errors)

### 5. The Meta-Proposal (Proposal 7)
The historical track record of TRL -1/-2 ideas eventually pointing at something real (Democritus → nuclear weapons, Hobbes → AGI, Wells → Hiroshima) suggests the practice has a non-zero hit rate over civilisational timescales. This observation is itself a TRL -1 proposal — self-referentially, the idea that ontological engineering works is itself an ontological engineering idea.

### 6. The AI Acceleration Corollary
If an AI could generate millions of proposals at TRL -1/-2 and filter for structural validity, the absolute number of hits could be non-trivial even with a tiny per-proposal hit rate. This is not "AI solves physics" — it's "AI generates ideas at a level of abstraction where humans can barely operate, at a scale no human could match, and one of them might turn out to be pointing at something as transformative as nuclear weapons."

### 7. Claude's Failed Attempt
Claude attempted to generate a proposal ("The Incompleteness Exploit" — Gödel's theorems applied to physics via Tegmark's Mathematical Universe Hypothesis). Sammy diagnosed it as a colourful metaphor disguised as an inference: singularities are DERIVED within GR (proven by the singularity theorems), while Gödel sentences are things a system CANNOT derive. Surface resemblance, not logical connection. TRL -3 at best.

### 8. The Campfire Phenomenology
Key insight about TRL -3: ideas at this level don't feel unlikely. They feel meaningless. They register as category errors — like saying "let's eat the colour blue." The stars-contain-fire intuition wasn't a bad idea to a prehistoric human; it wasn't an idea at all. And this is exactly how TRL -2 proposals feel to contemporaries. The indistinguishability between genuine insights and nonsense at the bottom of the ladder is a fundamental feature, not a bug.

### 9. The Lecture Introduction
Sammy asked Claude to write a fictional introduction to a graduate course on ontological engineering. The introduction uses the historical precedents (Democritus, Hobbes, Wells/Szilard) to establish the pattern, then introduces the negative TRL scale, and ends with the self-referential kick: "This course is itself a TRL -1 proposal. It might be nonsense. But it has worked before. And at TRL -1, that's enough."
