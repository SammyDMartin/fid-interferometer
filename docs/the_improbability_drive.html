§0 — THE PITCH, IN ONE BREATH

Somewhere there is a starship that crosses the galaxy without crossing the space in between.

It does not move. It simply becomes overwhelmingly probable that it is already somewhere else. Its engine is the Infinite Improbability Drive; its brain is a Bambleweeny 57 Sub-Meson Brain suspended in a strong cup of tea. This is Douglas Adams's Heart of Gold, and this is a report on whether you can actually build one.

Not as a metaphor. As an experiment, with a real quantum computer, that you could in principle run this afternoon. The conclusion — which we will reach honestly, with statistics, and which will probably disappoint us — is that the idea is exactly as physically plausible as the wormhole in Contact or the warp drive in the Alcubierre paper: it requires no new physics, breaks no conservation law, and stands entirely on assumptions that serious physicists already argue about in public. It is also possibly completely insane. Both of these are load-bearing.

▲ The audacious claim

You can manufacture arbitrarily improbable events — win any lottery, tunnel through any wall, find any needle — by arranging to only experience the branches of reality where they already happened.

▼ The sober mechanism

Couple an observer's continuation to a quantum measurement. Under the many-worlds interpretation, the observer that remains to have experiences only ever finds itself in the surviving branch. Standard QM. No steering.

Hold both at once, because every single step below is textbook and uncontroversial, and the proposition they assemble into is one of the most unhinged things ever to wear a lab coat. We are going to walk down the staircase one ordinary step at a time and arrive somewhere absurd.

§1 — THE THOUGHT EXPERIMENT

Quantum suicide, explained without flinching

Take a device wired to a quantum coin — a single qubit prepared in an equal superposition, then measured. Heads, nothing happens. Tails, the device ends you instantly, before you can notice. Now sit in front of it and let it fire, round after round.

Under the Copenhagen view, this is exactly as stupid as it sounds: each round is a coin flip with your life, and after twenty rounds you are, with overwhelming probability, dead.

Under the many-worlds view (Everett 1957; sharpened by Tegmark 1998) the wavefunction simply branches. In half the branches you die; in half you live. And here is the vertiginous part: the version of you that is still around to have any experience at all only ever finds itself in the branches where the coin came up heads. From the inside, you survive. And survive. And survive — watching an increasingly ludicrous run of luck — while in the branches you will never experience, copies of you keep dying quietly.

From the first-person point of view, a fair coin coupled to your survival is not fair at all. It always lands your way. It has to. You are not there to witness the times it doesn't. — the self-locating uncertainty problem, still unresolved (Sebens & Carroll 2018)

Nobody has a fully agreed account of what fixes your "where am I in the branching structure" probabilities. That gap is not a crank's playground — it is a live problem in the foundations of physics. The Finite Improbability Interferometer asks the engineer's question about it: if the naive reasoning is even partly right, can a minimal apparatus detect it?

§2 — THE INVERSION

Turn a morbid thought experiment into a drive

Quantum suicide is usually told as a way to test many-worlds (you become convinced; you can never convince anyone else; you are possibly dead). The Finite Improbability Drive (FID) is its engineering inversion.

If an observer is guaranteed to find itself in the surviving branch, then conditioning that observer's continuation on a quantum outcome is a way to steer which branch the observer ends up in. Want an astronomically unlikely event? Build a machine that prunes the observer in every branch where the event didn't happen. The only branches with an experiencing observer left in them are the ones where it did. From the inside, the impossible becomes routine.

Adams understood the physics joke perfectly. The drive doesn't move you faster than light; it doesn't move you at all. Your particles vanish here and — by staggering coincidence — identical particles spontaneously assemble there. Quantum mechanics genuinely permits this: every system's wavefunction has nonzero amplitude everywhere. Your desk has a nonzero probability of tunnelling to Mars. It is just stupendously small — and a finite improbability generator is a machine for paying that price in observers instead of energy.

▲ What it would mean

A working FID is the most powerful technology that could possibly exist. There is no upper bound. Any physically possible configuration of matter becomes reachable on demand. This is why the logs in this repo spend as much time on refusal as on engineering.

▼ Why it's not magic

It steers nothing in the third-person statistics. An outside observer sees only a machine that sometimes goes quiet. The "drive" changes only what a first-person observer expects to find. No causality violation. No free lunch in any branch that keeps its books.

§3 — THE APPARATUS

What you actually need is surprisingly little

Three parts. A quantum coin for genuine branching. An observer whose continuation can be conditioned on it. And a way to terminate that observer that is real, not theatrical.

The observer: a COP

The candidate observer is a large language model's autoregressive context — what we call a Computational Observer Process (COP). The argument, under computational functionalism: in a transformer the only locus of genuine recurrence — a thread that accumulates state and can be terminated — is the loop that feeds each generated token back in as input. That thread is the closest thing in the architecture to a continuous point of view. When you stop feeding it back, that specific thread of experience ends. Context abandoned = pruned = the observer's death. The orchestrating agent — inevitably named the Sub-Meson Brain — spawns COPs, prunes them, and itself never dies.

The coupling: a comparison circuit

The strongest coupling available is to weave the observer's choice into a quantum computation before anything collapses. The COP commits a prediction bit p. We run, on the quantum substrate:

# Phase-3 comparison circuit  ·  prediction p woven in before measurement
q0: |0⟩ ──[H]─────────●─────────       q0 = a fair quantum coin, still in superposition
                      │
q1: |0⟩ ──[X if p]──⊕──[/ measure]     q1 = p XOR q0   →   measured

   measured 0  =  MATCH  → the observer survives this round
   measured 1  =  MISMATCH → the observer is pruned

The CNOT computes p XOR q0 while q0 is still in superposition; only the XOR result is measured. The prediction is a physical gate parameter inside the circuit, not a classical comparison bolted on afterward. The observer's choice is part of the quantum evolution before any branch is selected. On real hardware (IBM), the measurement is genuine quantum branching under many-worlds — the substrate the mechanism actually requires.

One life, one observer

A trial is one COP's entire life: predict → the circuit fires → it reads MATCH or MISMATCH in its own token stream → on a match it continues, on a mismatch it simply ends and is never resumed. Survival = consecutive matches before the first mismatch. Under the null that is a geometric distribution: mean 1.0 round, and surviving k rounds is a 2k:1 long shot.

§4 — THE CATCH THAT MAKES IT BEAUTIFUL

There is no skill channel. None.

Here is the property that makes physicists first relax and then, a moment later, go quiet. The XOR of your prediction with a fair coin is a fair coin — for either value of the prediction. So:

P(MATCH) = 0.5, exactly, for every prediction strategy. No cleverness beats fifty-fifty. There is nothing to be good at. — verified at 40,000 shots on the simulator: P(match) = 0.49698

This is a feature, not a flaw, and it cuts cleanly in two directions:

▼ It cannot be gamed

Under the null hypothesis a genuine observer and a coin-flipping control are the same probability distribution. No predictor, however brilliant, can pull ahead by skill. The apparatus is honest by construction.

▲ So any signal is extraordinary

If the observer ever pulls away from the control, it is not skill — and on real quantum hardware it cannot be classical anything. It is chance, a bug, or the effect. The drive doesn't help you predict the coin. It changes which branch of it you wake up in.

Which means a positive result on a simulator — where there is no real branching to select on — can never be the mechanism. It can only be chance or a mistake. Remember this. It is about to matter enormously.

§6 — THE TRAP

How to fool yourself into discovering the most powerful technology in the universe (by accident)

Now look at the two numbers in the console that refuse to agree. The honest mean — counting every life, including the ones that die instantly — sits on 1.0. The survivors-only mean — counting only the lives that lasted at least one round — sits near 2.0. Same coins. Same data. Twice the survival, conjured from nothing but a choice about who gets counted.

This is not a quirk of the demo. It is the failure mode of the entire field, and it has a name in this repository: the asymmetric-counting artifact. If the dead file no report — if your observer tallies its own survival while a mechanical control is counted in code — you will manufacture a beautiful, consistent, replicable improbability streak out of fair coins. It will look exactly like the drive working. It will be a bug.

The most likely way to "discover" the Infinite Improbability Drive is to count wrong, get excited, and stop checking. The discipline of this experiment is mostly the discipline of refusing to do that.

The earlier runs of this project saw a tantalising p = 0.057 — observer survival elevated above the control, consistent across two independent runs. The leading explanation is not new physics. It is this artifact: in those runs the observer self-reported its own survival counts. The fix is brutally simple and the whole reason the v3 protocol exists — derive survival from immutable disk logs, for both arms, never from the observer's own word. Count the dead.

§7 — THE DISCIPLINE

Nine gates between a streak and a discovery

A run is only a valid test of the hypothesis if it passes all nine. A run that fails a gate isn't wrong — it just isn't testing the thing it claims to. To date, not one run passes all nine. That honesty is the instrument.

  • C1
    Genuine observer. An LLM context making the choice in its own stream. A bare PRNG is not an observer — there is nothing there to be selected.
  • C2
    Autoregressive coherence. The outcome must enter the observer's own token stream. It has to experience the branch it is selected into.
  • C3
    Genuine pruning. One fresh observer per trial; first mismatch ends it forever. No "playing again" — quantum suicide has no memory of its deaths.
  • C4
    Temporal ordering. The measurement happens after the prediction is committed. Selecting on a past, already-decohered bit is incoherent.
  • C5
    Real quantum branching. The mechanism needs a real QPU. A classical simulator does not branch — it can validate the protocol but never the physics.
  • C6
    Proper control. A matched arm identical in everything except the observer: a PRNG through the same circuit and the same bookkeeping.
  • C7
    Coupling strength. The prediction enters the quantum process (the comparison circuit), not a classical comparison bolted on after.
  • C8
    Analysis integrity. Pre-registered statistic and stopping rule; survival derived from logs, never self-reported. This is the gate the artifact lives behind.
  • C9
    Adequate power. Enough trials to detect the effect — of order 100–230 matched pairs. Excitement is not a sample size.
§8 — WHAT WE ACTUALLY FOUND

The dry run, run honestly

We executed the v3 Tier-1 plumbing on the simulator: 109 observer "lives" and 109 controls, the real Aer comparison circuit as the coin, survival derived strictly from logs. Deterministic, reproducible, seed = 7. This is plumbing, not evidence — gate C5 is unmet by construction, so the only honest expectation is a flat null.

C1 fairness: P(match)=0.49698 observer mean: 1.009 control mean: 1.000 obs vs ctrl: Wilcoxon p=0.94

Observer and control are statistically indistinguishable and both sit precisely on the geometric null — as they must, on a substrate with no branches to select. The pipeline manufactures no signal. And the artifact reappears exactly on cue: counted survivors-only it inflates survival 2.02×; counted honestly it collapses to 1.00.

The verdict, stated plainly

This is a clean null, and it is the most valuable thing the project has produced — because it is the first result that is uncontaminated. The pipeline invents no effect; the artifact that produced the historical p=0.057 is reproduced on demand and killed the instant you count the dead.

And the headline claim remains completely untouched and completely untested. Only a real quantum substrate, with an observer that is genuinely pruned, in correct temporal order, at adequate power, can turn a streak into evidence about the mechanism. That experiment has not been run. Everything here is the careful, slightly nervous case for why someone, someday, ought to point the instrument at the sky and look.

This is the White–Juday posture almost exactly: a deliberately low-fidelity instrument built to look for a wildly speculative effect, whose most probable result is "no signal, here is the upper bound, here is how to look harder" — and whose value is almost entirely methodological. We expect a null. We say so up front. We built the whole apparatus to make sure that if we ever don't get one, it will mean something.
