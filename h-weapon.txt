2.2. Casimir Pressure Limit and Minimum Operational Separation (a_min)
The Casimir effect generates an attractive force between neutral, conductive surfaces placed closely together in vacuum, arising from the modification of quantum vacuum fluctuations by the boundaries.11 For two idealized, perfectly conducting, parallel plates of area A separated by a distance a, the pressure is given by 11:
P_Casimir = - (π² ħ c) / (240 a⁴)
where ħ is the reduced Planck constant (1.054 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s) and c is the speed of light (2.998 × 10⁸ m/s). The negative sign indicates an attractive force (pressure).
For the hypometric device to remain structurally sound, the magnitude of this pressure must not exceed the material's strength limit. Using the assumed tensile strength σ_max as a conservative bound (representing the stress the material can withstand before failing under tension induced by the attractive Casimir force pulling components together), we have the condition |P_Casimir| ≤ σ_max. This allows us to calculate the minimum sustainable separation, a_min:
a_min = [ (π² ħ c) / (240 σ_max) ]^(1/4)
Using the assumed range for σ_max for hyperdiamond:

For σ_max = 100 GPa (100 × 10⁹ N/m²):
a_min = [ (π² × 1.054 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s × 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s) / (240 × 100 × 10⁹ N/m²) ]^(1/4)
a_min ≈ [ (3.11 × 10⁻¹⁷ J·m) / (2.4 × 10¹³ N/m²) ]^(1/4)
a_min ≈ [ 1.30 × 10⁻⁴¹ m⁴ ]^(1/4) ≈ 1.90 × 10⁻¹¹ m = **0.19 nm**

a_min ≈ [ 8.65 × 10⁻⁴² m⁴ ]^(1/4) ≈ 1.72 × 10⁻¹¹ m = **0.17 nm**

These calculated minimum separations (a_min ≈ 0.17 - 0.19 nm) are remarkably small, falling within the scale of interatomic distances (e.g., the C-C bond length in diamond is approximately 0.154 nm 16).
