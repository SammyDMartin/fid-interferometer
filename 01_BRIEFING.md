# Ontological Engineering: Briefing Document

## What This Is

Ontological engineering is a practice that sits below the floor of Drexler's exploratory engineering. Where exploratory engineering designs hypothetical systems within known physics (TRL 1-2), ontological engineering generates ideas at TRL -1 to -2: logical inferences from contested or uncertain frameworks whose premises might not describe reality at all.

The practice was identified and named by Sammy Martin, a researcher at Founders Pledge who works on AI existential risk and has an unusual cross-disciplinary background spanning physics (BSc Physics & Philosophy, Durham), AI (MSc AI, Edinburgh), philosophy of science, theology, and science fiction. His undergraduate thesis was on anthropic reasoning and the Doomsday Argument. He has developed several original concepts in this space over several years.

## The Core Intellectual Move

Take a framework's axioms — whether from physics, theology, metaphysics, philosophy of fiction, or anthropic reasoning — and treat them as **engineering specifications** rather than abstract principles. Then ask: what does this framework permit that its designers didn't intend? Where are the vulnerabilities? What can you exploit?

This is analogous to penetration testing in software security, but applied to worldviews and metaphysical frameworks. The Eucharist Hack is a theological exploit. The Omni-Experiment is a metaphysical exploit. The Improbability Drive analysis is reverse-engineering an exploit from fiction and discovering it almost compiles.

## Why Most Attempts Fail

A valid ontological engineering proposal must have **actual logical force** — it must follow from the premises of the framework it exploits. The most common failure mode (demonstrated in this very conversation when Claude attempted one) is producing something that **pattern-matches on the style** of a valid proposal without having the substance. Specifically:

- **Colourful metaphors disguised as inferences.** Noticing that two things share a surface feature (e.g., "singularities and Gödel sentences both involve the system breaking down") and treating this as a logical connection. It's not. The resemblance is cosmetic. Singularities are derived within GR using GR's own formalism; Gödel sentences are specifically things a system CANNOT derive. These are opposite phenomena dressed in similar language.
- **TRL -3 masquerading as TRL -1.** If your proposal amounts to "X and Y both involve limits, therefore they're connected," you're at mythic intuition level, not framework exploitation. A valid proposal needs a specific mechanism or logical chain, not an analogy.

## What Success Looks Like

A successful proposal:
1. Identifies a specific framework (Catholic transubstantiation doctrine, Many-Worlds + anthropic reasoning, Parfitian no-selector metaphysics, etc.)
2. Takes its axioms literally and completely seriously
3. Follows the internal logic to a conclusion the framework's proponents haven't noticed or have avoided
4. Produces a conclusion that sounds insane but is **logically valid conditional on the premises**
5. Cannot be dismissed without rejecting one of the premises (and the premises are ones the framework's own proponents accept)

The comedy and the power come from the same source: the gap between how seriously the framework takes itself and how wild the conclusions are when you actually follow through. This is the same structure as Swift's *Modest Proposal* — the economic logic of treating Irish poor as livestock is internally sound given mercantilist premises. The horror is structural, not decorative.

## Historical Hit Rate

The overwhelming majority of ideas at TRL -1 and -2 lead nowhere. But the historical hit rate is non-zero, and the ideas that hit tend to be civilisation-defining:

- Democritus (atoms and void, ~400 BCE) → nuclear weapons (1945): ~2,400 years
- Hobbes ("reasoning is reckoning," 1651) → artificial general intelligence (202X): ~375 years
- Wells (atomic bombs in fiction, 1913) → Hiroshima (1945): 32 years
- Szilard (chain reaction concept, 1933) → Chicago Pile-1 (1942): 9 years

The practice of generating TRL -1 to -2 ideas is the engineering equivalent of what philosophy does when it matures into science. Mature ontological engineering becomes real engineering, the same way mature philosophy becomes science.
